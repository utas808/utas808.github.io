What does ʻĀina mean?

ʻĀina means land, but its meaning is deeper than just the ground we live on. It includes the natural environment that provides food, water, and resources. Many Native Hawaiians understand the ʻāina as something to respect, care for, and protect.

Mauka to Makai

Mauka means toward the mountains, and makai means toward the ocean. Water and resources move through these connected areas, showing why caring for the whole environment is important.

Lo'i Kalo and Traditional Farming

Lo'i kalo are wetland fields used to grow kalo (taro). Kalo is an important traditional food and has deep cultural meaning for Native Hawaiians. Growing it required knowledge of water, soil, and the environment.

Protecting the ʻĀina Today

People can care for the ʻāina be reducing waste, protecting native plants and aminals, conserving water, and respecting natural and cultural sites. Caring for the land helps protect resources for future generations.

Why It Matters

The relationship between people and the ʻāina is an important part of Hawaiian history and culture. Understanding this relationship helps us apprecuate traditional knowledge and the responsibility to care for Hawai'i
