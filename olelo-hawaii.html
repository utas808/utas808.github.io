What is ʻŌlelo Hawai'i

ʻŌlelo Hawai'i is the Native Hawaiin language. It carries the history, knowledge, values, and identity of the Hawaiian people. The languag is closely connected to Hawaiian culture and traditions.

The Hawaiian Alphabet

The Hawaiin alphabet has 13 letters: Five vowels (A E I O U) and eight consonants (H K L M N P W and the ʻokina). The ʻokina represents a golttal stop, while the kahakō marks a long vowel.

Common Hawaiian Words

  • Aloha:Love, affection, greeting, or farewells
  • Mahalo:Thank you or gratitude
  • ʻĀina:Land
  • Kai:Sea or ocean
  • Mālama:To care for or protect.
  • Kūpuna:Elders or ancestors

Language Suppression and Revitalizations

After the illegal overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom, the use of Hawaiian in schools was taken away by an 1896 law. Over time, the number of fluent speakers declined. Hawaiian language advocates an communities worked hard to bring the language back through immerson schools, education, and community programs.

Keeping the Language Alive

People help preserve ʻŌlelo Hawai'i by learning and speaking it, teaching it to younger generations, and using it in everyday life. Every word learned helps strengthen the connection between language, culture, and identity.
