Our Eight Main Islands

The Hawaiian Islands are located in the Pacific Ocean. They were formed by volcanic activity and are known for their unique landscapes, plants, and cultures. Native Hawaiians have strong connections to the land and sea.

Meet the Islands

Hawai'i Island (Big Island)

Hawai'i Island is the largest island in the chain. It is home to active volcanoes, including Kīlauea and Mauna Loa.

Maui

Maui is known for Haleakalā, beautiful beaches, and the historic town of Lahaina.

O'ahu

O'ahu is home to Honolulu, the state capital, more beautiful areas, and important historical places such as Pearl Habor.

Kaua'i/h3>

Kaua'i is known for its green mountains, valleys, and the beautiful Nāpali Coast.

Moloka'i

Moloka'i is know for its strong community ties and its deep connections to Native Hawaiian traditions

Lānaʻi

Lānaʻi is known for its quiet landscapes and history of pineapple farming.

Ni'ihau

Ni'ihau is privately owned and is known for protecting the Hawaiian language and traditionals ways of life.

Kaho'olawe

Kaho'olawe is uninhabitated and has great cultural significance, restoration efforts aim to protect and heal the island.

Why the Islands Matter

Each island has its own history, environment, and communities. Learning about them helps us understand the relationship between Native Hawaiians and the ʻāina, or land.
