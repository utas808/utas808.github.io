The First Voyagers

The first Native Hawaiian ancestors were Polynesian voyagers who traveled across the Pacific Ocean in canoes. They used stars, winds, waves, and ocean currents to navigate long distances.

Life in Early Hawai'i

Early Hawaiians developed communities across the islands. They grew crops such as kalo(taro), raised animals, fished, and gathered resources from the land and sea.

The Ahupua'a System

An ahupua'a was a traditional land division that often extended from the mountains to the ocean. It connected different natural resources and helped communities care for the land and share what they needed.

Ali'i, Kahuna, and Makaʻāinana

Traditional Hawaiian society included ali'i (chiefs), kahuna (experts and religious practitioners), and makaʻāinana (common people). Different roles helped communities organize daily life and responsibilities.

Why Early Hawaiian History Matters

Learning about early Hawai'i helps us understand the knowledge, skills, values, and traditions passed down through generations. These connections remain important to Hawaiian identity and culture today.
