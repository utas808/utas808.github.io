Hula

Hula is a traditional Hawaiian dance that shares stories, history, and feelings through movement and expression. Hula is connected to mele (songs and chants) and helps pass cultural knowledge from one generation to another.

Mele

Mele are Hawaiian songs and chants. They can tell stories about people, places, nature, love, and important events. Mele helps preserve Hawaiian history, values, and language.

Mo'olelo

Mo'olelo are stories, histories, and accounts passed down thorugh generations. They share knowledge about ancestors, imporant places, and Hawaiians beliefs and traditions.

Lei Making

Lei's are made using flowers, leaves, shells, and other materials. They are often give to show love, respect, appreciation, and celebration.

ʻOhana and Community

ʻOhana means family and includes relationship that support people and communities. Caring for others, respecting kūpuna (elders), and sharing knowledge are important values in many Hawaiian families.

Keeping Traditions Alive

Learning ʻōlelo Hawai'i, practicing hula, sharing mo'olelo, and caring for the ʻāina help keep Hawaiian culture strong. These traditions connect people to their ancestors and cultural identity.
